Abstract

This thesis presents a structured finance and institutional governance framework for the Afro-Indian Strategic Minerals & Capital Corridor (AIA-SMCC), a three-node mineral trade infrastructure programme connecting Horn of Africa producing regions to Indian industrial and financial markets via a Dubai transshipment hub. The programme is branded as the Sovereign SafeTrade Program (IMEC-SSTP) and is designed to align with the India-Middle East-Europe Economic Corridor (IMEC) policy architecture.

The analysis examines: (i) the jurisdictional and regulatory architecture required for compliant multi-node mineral settlement; (ii) the role of development finance institutions (DFC, MIGA, EXIM, USTDA) in de-risking corridor infrastructure; (iii) the application of the Dual-Anchor Settlement Protocol (DASP v2.0) as a multi-rail clearing mechanism; and (iv) the political risk insurance framework required for operations in Somaliland, UAE, and Bihar, India.

Keywords: AIA-SMCC · IMEC-SSTP · SafeTrade Program · DASP v2.0 · DFC · MIGA · LBMA · FATF · R3 Corda · Somaliland · Bihar · Structured Finance · Political Risk Insurance

Chapter 1

Introduction: The Mineral-to-Market Gap

Sub-Saharan Africa holds an estimated 30% of the world's known mineral reserves, yet receives a disproportionately small share of the value created from those reserves. The primary driver of this value gap is not geological — it is institutional: the absence of compliant, transparent, LBMA-aligned supply chains that can connect African mineral production to the world's premium markets at verified quality standards.

India presents a particular opportunity. As the world's second-largest consumer of gold and a major industrial consumer of silver, India imports the overwhelming majority of its precious metals demand through opaque, multi-tiered intermediary chains. The demonetization shock of 2016, the Goods and Services Tax restructure, and the formalization of India's bullion market through the India International Bullion Exchange (IIBX) at GIFT City have created structural demand for a direct, verified, DFI-backed supply corridor.

"The Sovereign SafeTrade Program is designed to close the institutional gap between African mineral wealth and Indian industrial demand — not by adding another intermediary, but by replacing the intermediary layer with sovereign infrastructure."

This thesis argues that the AIA-SMCC, structured as a three-node public-private partnership with DFI backing, FATF-compliant KYC/AML architecture, and the DASP v2.0 multi-rail settlement protocol, represents a replicable institutional model for verified commodity corridor development under the IMEC framework.

Chapter 2

The IMEC Framework and AIA-SMCC Policy Alignment

The India-Middle East-Europe Economic Corridor (IMEC) was announced at the G20 New Delhi Summit in September 2023. The corridor envisions a network of shipping lanes, rail connections, and trade facilitation infrastructure connecting India to the Gulf states and onward to Europe — with a particular emphasis on reducing dependency on China-dominated supply chains.

2.1 AIA-SMCC as IMEC Extension Corridor

While IMEC's primary axis runs India–Gulf–Europe, the AIA-SMCC operates as a feeder corridor to the IMEC framework — bringing Horn of Africa mineral production into the IMEC settlement system via the Dubai transshipment hub. This positions Berbera (Somaliland) as a strategic IMEC-adjacent mineral aggregation point.

Chapter 3

Three-Node Infrastructure Architecture

3.1 Node 1 — SSTLC: Berbera Economic Zone, Somaliland

The Sovereign SafeTrade Logistics Complex (SSTLC) is the corridor's mineral intake point. Berbera's geographic position — on the Gulf of Aden, 20km from the international shipping lane — makes it a natural aggregation hub for Horn of Africa mineral production from Ethiopia, Eritrea, Djibouti, and Somalia-adjacent producing regions.

The SSTLC's core compliance architecture relies on:

  • OECD Due Diligence Guidance for Responsible Supply Chains of Minerals from Conflict-Affected and High-Risk Areas (CAHRA guidance)
  • Third-party assaying by LBMA-accredited or OECD-certified assayers
  • Chain of custody documentation from point of intake to LBMA Good Delivery certification
  • FATF risk-based approach for Somaliland's unrecognized territory status

3.2 Node 2 — Dubai Transshipment Hub: JAFZA/DMCC

The Dubai hub serves as the corridor's value-added processing and re-export node. JAFZA (Jebel Ali Free Zone Authority) provides the physical and regulatory infrastructure; DMCC (Dubai Multi Commodities Centre) provides the bullion trading and pricing framework.

3.3 Node 3 — BIBC: Bihar International Bullion Centre

The Bihar International Bullion Centre (BIBC) is the corridor's India terminus. Structured as a public-private partnership under Bihar's Industrial Policy & PPP framework (BIPPP-2025), the BIBC is designed to become an LBMA Good Delivery refiner — the first in Bihar state — with sovereign vaulting and bullion banking capabilities integrated with GIFT City's IFSCA regulatory sandbox.

The Bihar VGF ($10M, 0% concessional loan) approved by DFC (EV-0088) represents the first DFI commitment to the programme and validates the BIBC's eligibility for further DFC Political Risk Insurance coverage.

Chapter 5

Political Risk Insurance Framework

The AIA-SMCC's three-node architecture spans three distinct political risk environments. Each node requires tailored PRI coverage from jurisdiction-appropriate providers.

5.1 BIBC (Bihar, India) — U.S. DFC

India is an OPIC-eligible country and a preferred DFC partner. DFC PRI for BIBC covers expropriation, political violence, and currency inconvertibility up to $250M per project. The DFC VGF approval (EV-0088) establishes institutional relationship precedent for the PRI application.

5.2 SSTLC (Somaliland) — MIGA

Somaliland's unrecognized territory status requires MIGA coverage rather than bilateral DFC coverage. MIGA's Conflict-Affected and Fragile Economies Facility (CAFE) is the appropriate vehicle. Coverage addresses expropriation, war/civil disturbance, and breach of concession agreement.

5.3 Dubai Hub (UAE) — Lloyd's of London

The UAE carries manageable political risk relative to the other nodes. Lloyd's political risk syndicate coverage is appropriate for trade disruption, license revocation, and sanctions entanglement risk.

Chapter 7

Conclusions & Policy Recommendations

The AIA-SMCC / Sovereign SafeTrade Program represents a new institutional archetype for verified commodity corridor development: one that combines public-private partnership structure, multi-DFI co-investment, FATF-compliant compliance architecture, and proprietary multi-rail settlement technology (DASP v2.0) in a single integrated framework.

The programme's critical success factors are:

  1. Bihar BIPPP-2025 formalization — the critical path item for Phase 0 completion (deadline 2026-03-31)
  2. DFC PRI concept note acceptance — validates the BIBC investment thesis for institutional co-investors
  3. Somaliland EoI acceptance — unlocks the SSTLC node and MIGA PRI eligibility
  4. DASP v2.0 technical completion — enables live settlement operations across all three nodes
  5. IFSCA GIFT City sandbox approval — provides India regulatory framework for BIBC bullion banking

The programme's alignment with IMEC policy objectives, its DFI-backed capital structure, and its LBMA/FATF/OECD compliance architecture position it as a model for the next generation of Africa-to-Asia commodity corridor infrastructure under the PGII framework.

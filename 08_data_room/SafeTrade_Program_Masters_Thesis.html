Prefatory

Abstract

This thesis examines the design, regulatory architecture, and strategic positioning of the SafeTrade Program — a private-sector initiative to establish a regulated, DLT-enabled gold and commodities trade corridor linking Somaliland, the UAE, and Bihar, India within the framework of the India-Middle East-Europe Economic Corridor (IMEC). The research evaluates the DASP v2.0 settlement architecture, multi-jurisdictional legal structure, US government engagement strategy (DFC/MIGA), and brand governance framework across the SignatureSovereign, IndiSovereign, and SovereignSomaliland sub-brands. The thesis argues that the SafeTrade corridor represents a viable, institutionally-compliant model for private-sector IMEC corridor infrastructure under existing BUILD Act 2018 and MIGA treaty frameworks.
Chapter One

Introduction — The IMEC Opportunity

The India-Middle East-Europe Economic Corridor (IMEC) was announced at the G20 New Delhi Summit in September 2023 as a multilateral infrastructure initiative connecting India, the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Jordan, Israel, and Europe via railway and shipping lanes. The corridor represents the largest multimodal infrastructure announcement since the Belt and Road Initiative.

The SafeTrade Program positions Signature Sovereign Solutions LLC as a private-sector institutional node within this corridor, specifically serving the underexplored Horn of Africa / Berbera vector — connecting Somaliland's mineral resources and Berbera BEZ export zone through the Dubai hub to Bihar's rapidly developing industrial ecosystem.

Research Questions

This thesis addresses three primary research questions:

1. Can a private-sector entity establish a commercially viable, regulatory-compliant DLT-enabled gold trade corridor within the IMEC framework under existing US, Indian, UAE, and Somaliland regulatory regimes?

2. What settlement architecture (DASP v2.0) is necessary and sufficient to satisfy institutional counterparty requirements (LBMA, DFC, correspondent banks) while maintaining operational efficiency?

3. What multi-jurisdictional legal entity structure optimally balances investor protection, regulatory compliance, and operational flexibility across four jurisdictions?

Chapter Two

Corridor Architecture & Strategic Positioning

The Berbera Vector

Somaliland's Berbera Business & Export Zone (BEZ) represents a strategically underutilized entry point into the IMEC corridor. DP World holds a 30-year operational concession for the Berbera port — one of the most significant private infrastructure investments in the Horn of Africa. The port's deep-water capacity (12.5m draft) positions it to serve Capesize and Panamax bulk carriers transporting gold concentrate, industrial minerals, and agricultural commodities.

The USGS Mineral Resources Data System (MRDS) contains records for Somaliland's mineral endowment within the Mozambique Orogenic Belt — a geological formation known for gold, REE, and base metal mineralization.

Bihar Industrial Ecosystem

Bihar represents one of India's most dynamic emerging industrial investment destinations under the state government's industrial promotion policy framework (BIPPP-2025). The state has established targeted incentive programs for fintech, logistics, and manufacturing investment, with the Bihar FinTech City initiative as a key anchor for corridor operations.

Chapter Three

Regulatory Framework & US Government Engagement

DFC (US International Development Finance Corporation)

The DFC operates under the BUILD Act of 2018 with a current authorization ceiling of $60 billion. The corporation provides political risk insurance, debt financing, equity financing, and technical assistance for projects in developing countries that advance US foreign policy objectives.

The SafeTrade corridor aligns with multiple DFC priority areas: infrastructure in fragile states (Somaliland), critical minerals (Mozambique Belt), and Indo-Pacific economic connectivity (Bihar BIBC). Precedent transactions include the Lobito Railway (DRC/Angola), Orion minerals, and TechMet.

DFC Authorization Note: The DFC's current enacted ceiling under BUILD Act 2018 is $60 billion. Any reference to a $205 billion ceiling in programme materials represents a proposed/projected legislative expansion that has not been enacted as of March 2026. Programme applications are scoped within the existing $60B ceiling.

OFAC / Sanctions Compliance

Somaliland operations require careful OFAC compliance navigation. While Somaliland is not internationally recognized as a sovereign state, it is not subject to a general OFAC embargo in the same manner as the Federal Republic of Somalia. The programme maintains Legal Opinion LO-001 addressing OFAC compliance for Somaliland BEZ operations.

Reg D 506(c) Framework

The SSS SafeTrade Fund LP is structured as a Delaware limited partnership issuing securities under Reg D 506(c) to accredited investors only. All investors undergo verified accreditation per SEC Rule 501, with OFAC SDN, UN, EU sanctions screening and KYC/AML verification per FinCEN and FATF standards.

Conclusion

Conclusion & Programme Outlook

The SafeTrade Program represents a viable, first-mover private-sector initiative within the IMEC corridor framework. The DASP v2.0 architecture resolves the regulatory barriers that prevented institutional adoption of the v1.0 (Bitcoin/Lightning/Ethereum) framework. The multi-jurisdictional entity structure provides adequate ring-fencing and regulatory compliance across four jurisdictions.

Critical near-term milestones include: Bihar BIPPP-2025 application (deadline 2026-03-31), DFC concept note submission (Q2 2026), SSTLC BEZ registration (Q2 2026), and IFSCA sandbox application (Q3 2026). The programme's viability is contingent on successful execution of these milestones within the stated timeframes.

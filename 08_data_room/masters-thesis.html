Abstract

This thesis presents the design, legal architecture, and corridor economics of the Afro-Indian Strategic Minerals & Capital Corridor (AI-SMCC) — a bilateral trade infrastructure initiative by Signature Sovereign Solutions LLC operating under the public brand SafeTrade Program. The corridor connects India's emerging minerals refining capacity in Bihar with Somaliland's documented mineral endowment via the UAE's Jebel Ali Free Zone, architecturally aligned with the India-Middle East-Europe Economic Corridor (IMEC) announced at the G20 New Delhi Summit in September 2023.

The research documents the evolution from a first-generation digital asset settlement platform (DASP v1.0) employing Bitcoin and public Ethereum to an institutionally compliant second-generation architecture (DASP v2.0) utilizing R3 Corda, SWIFT gpi, and Fnality International. The thesis analyzes the 7-entity legal cascade across 4 jurisdictions, the compliance stack (Reg D 506(c), OFAC/SDN, AML/KYC), and the political risk mitigation framework (DFC, MIGA).

Scenario models project corridor economics under a March 2026 reference scenario and identify key milestones for corridor activation.

Chapter One

Strategic Context & Research Motivation

1.1 The IMEC Framework

The India-Middle East-Europe Economic Corridor (IMEC) was announced at the G20 Leaders Summit in New Delhi on September 9–10, 2023. The corridor represents a multi-modal infrastructure network connecting India, the Middle East, and Europe through rail, maritime, and digital connectivity infrastructure.

"The IMEC will stimulate economic development through enhanced connectivity and economic integration between Asia, the Arabian Gulf, and Europe." — White House Fact Sheet, September 9, 2023

While IMEC focuses primarily on energy, digital infrastructure, and manufactured goods corridors, a critical gap exists in the formal channeling of strategic minerals from the Horn of Africa through the Middle East corridor to South Asian processing facilities. This thesis documents the AI-SMCC as a private-sector implementation node designed to address this gap.

1.2 The Somaliland Minerals Endowment

The USGS Mineral Resources Data System (MRDS) contains approximately 72 records for the Somaliland/Somalia region, documenting deposits across the Mozambique Orogenic Belt. The geological context — ancient crystalline basement terranes with documented gold, silver, copper, and rare earth potential — is well-established in academic literature.

Despite this documented endowment, Somaliland's minerals sector remains largely underdeveloped due to: (1) unresolved international recognition status; (2) absence of institutional-grade compliance infrastructure for export; and (3) lack of certified refinery capacity within the corridor.

1.3 India's Import Demand

India represents one of the world's largest consumers of gold and silver. The Reserve Bank of India and World Gold Council data document surging gold imports in the 2024–2025 period, driven by retail demand, central bank accumulation, and hallmarking requirements.

The Bihar state government's BIPPP-2025 industrial promotion policy creates a unique window for establishing certified refinery capacity in Eastern India, with incentives including capital subsidy, SGST refunds, and land allocation.

2.2 Securities Law Framework

Capital formation activities operate under the Regulation D Rule 506(c) exemption from SEC registration, permitting general solicitation while restricting participation to Accredited Investors under Rule 501(a). This framework was selected for: (1) general solicitation permission; (2) no issuer size limits; and (3) established precedent in institutional private placements.

Chapter Three

3.1 DASP v1.0 — Initial Design Limitations

The first-generation Digital Asset Settlement Platform (DASP v1.0) employed Bitcoin/Lightning Network for micropayment settlement and public Ethereum for smart contract execution. A regulatory audit identified critical institutional barriers:

Bitcoin and public Ethereum face significant institutional adoption barriers including: FATF Travel Rule compliance complexity on public blockchains; lack of privacy controls required for institutional counterparties; regulatory uncertainty in key jurisdictions; and absence of institutional-grade custody solutions acceptable to DFC and MIGA.
